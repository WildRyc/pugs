Gardening Resources

Whether you're a beginner or experienced gardener, these resources will help you grow successfully in our coastal climate.

Getting Started

Planning Your Garden

  • Assess Your Space: Consider sunlight (6+ hours for vegetables), water access, and soil quality
  • Start Small: Begin with a few easy-to-grow crops like lettuce, radishes, and herbs
  • Choose the Right Plants: Select varieties suited to our coastal climate and growing season
  • Plan for Succession: Stagger plantings for continuous harvest throughout the season

Essential Tools

  • Hand trowel and cultivator
  • Garden fork and spade
  • Watering can or hose
  • Pruning shears
  • Garden gloves
  • Wheelbarrow or garden cart

Seasonal Planting Guide

Spring (March - May)

  • Early Spring: Peas, lettuce, spinach, arugula, radishes, onion sets
  • Mid Spring: Broccoli, cabbage, cauliflower, kale, Swiss chard
  • Late Spring: Beans, carrots, beets, potatoes

Summer (June - August)

  • Early Summer: Tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers, squash, zucchini
  • Mid Summer: Continue succession planting of beans and lettuce
  • Late Summer: Plant fall crops like broccoli, cabbage, and kale

Fall (September - November)

  • Early Fall: Lettuce, spinach, arugula, Asian greens
  • Mid Fall: Garlic for next year's harvest
  • Late Fall: Cover crops to enrich soil over winter

Winter (December - February)

  • Harvest hardy greens (kale, collards, Brussels sprouts)
  • Plan next year's garden
  • Order seeds and prepare materials
  • Maintain tools and equipment

Soil Health

Building Great Soil

  • Compost: Add 2-3 inches of finished compost annually
  • Mulch: Apply organic mulch to retain moisture and suppress weeds
  • Cover Crops: Plant in fall to prevent erosion and add nutrients
  • Crop Rotation: Rotate plant families to prevent disease and pest buildup

Composting Basics

  • Browns: Dry leaves, straw, paper (carbon-rich)
  • Greens: Vegetable scraps, grass clippings, coffee grounds (nitrogen-rich)
  • Balance: Mix roughly 3 parts brown to 1 part green
  • Moisture: Keep as moist as a wrung-out sponge
  • Air: Turn pile regularly to speed decomposition

Pest and Disease Management

Organic Pest Control

  • Prevention: Healthy soil = healthy plants = fewer pest problems
  • Companion Planting: Marigolds, nasturtiums, and herbs deter pests
  • Physical Barriers: Row covers, netting, and collars protect plants
  • Hand Picking: Remove pests manually when populations are small
  • Beneficial Insects: Encourage ladybugs, lacewings, and other predators

Common Problems

  • Aphids: Spray with water or use insecticidal soap
  • Slugs: Use beer traps, copper barriers, or hand pick in evening
  • Powdery Mildew: Improve air circulation, water at soil level
  • Blossom End Rot: Ensure consistent watering and adequate calcium

Water Management

  • Deep Watering: Water deeply and less frequently to encourage deep roots
  • Morning Watering: Water in early morning to reduce disease
  • Drip Irrigation: Consider drip systems for efficient water use
  • Mulching: Apply mulch to reduce evaporation
  • Rain Barrels: Collect rainwater for garden use
